### 3.3 Keep a slow snapshot connected

User action: In Android Studio, run the app unit tests (at least `SyncApplierTest`, `LanSessionTest` and `StallTraceTest`), then install the debug app on the phone and attach once. Report the test counts and whether the chip reaches Live.

The throttled runs in item 3.2 show each socket dying at about 15 s while snapshot pages were still arriving, after which the snapshot restarted from the beginning. In the Android app, `LanSession.deliveryStalled` ends a Live socket once `SyncRecoveryBudgetMs` (15 s) passes with no committed progress. `SyncApplier` reports committed progress only for applied events and at `SnapshotEnd`. A `SnapshotPage` is not counted. So a snapshot that takes longer than 15 s to arrive is cut off and restarts every time, and never finishes. This may not be the stall first reported on a fast LAN, but it blocks large histories on slow links.

Let validated snapshot pages that keep arriving count as progress for the delivery deadline. Keep a deadline for a socket that stops sending pages, and keep the overall ceiling. Do not let page progress advance the durable contiguous cursor. Add Android regressions: a snapshot whose pages arrive steadily over more than 15 s stays connected and reaches `SnapshotEnd`, and a socket that stops sending pages before `SnapshotEnd` is still ended at the deadline.

The first live attach in item 3.2 also logged `2479` State and `123` ResumeOk frames and no Event frames. Find what drives those repeated resumes, and fix it if it is a loop. Run the relevant Android tests and build, plus desktop monitor tests and package compilation if the desktop changes.

Found 2026-11-22 (real phone, idle desktop after the Lazarus rebuild): the phone showed the plan, the chip stayed on `Catching up`, and the conversation stayed blank. `OpenTurnFollow` in `SyncApplier.kt` raised the open turn's applied sequence only from live events. A baseline committed at `SnapshotEnd`, or a Room cursor already at the head on reconnect, never counted. An idle desktop sends no events after the baseline, so the open turn stayed behind the announced head indefinitely. After 15 s `openTurnRecovery` sent a resume, the desktop answered ResumeOk with nothing to replay, the back-off ended in a reconnect, and the cycle repeated. That is the State and ResumeOk churn above. The blank conversation is most likely each reconnect replacing the board before it paints; that part is not yet proven.
